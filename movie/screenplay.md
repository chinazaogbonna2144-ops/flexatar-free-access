# THE GIRL WHO HEARD HER DEAD MOTHER
### A 30-minute supernatural mystery short film
**Written for production planning**

## Main characters
- **AMARA (19):** Quiet, observant, still grieving her mother.
- **MAMA IFEOMA (voice, 40s):** Amara’s late mother. Loving, careful, and carrying a secret.
- **UNCLE EMEKA (40s):** Amara’s guardian. Protective, but afraid the past will surface.
- **NNEKA (19):** Amara’s candid best friend, who believes her fear deserves to be heard.

---

## SCENE 1 — THE HOUSE THAT LISTENS (NIGHT)

Rain falls over an old family house. The windows are dark except for a faint square of blue light upstairs. A loose shutter taps against the wall.

Inside, AMARA sleeps. A whisper slips through the room.

**MAMA IFEOMA (V.O.)**  
Amara.

Amara stirs. The whisper is almost lost beneath the rain.

**MAMA IFEOMA (V.O.)**  
My child.

Amara opens her eyes. Her room is empty. The door is closed. She sits up, listening.

**AMARA**  
Who’s there?

Only rain answers. She turns toward the family photograph on her bedside table: Amara as a child, held by her mother. The glass is cracked across her mother’s smile.

The whisper returns, closer now.

**MAMA IFEOMA (V.O.)**  
Don’t let him burn it.

Amara swings her feet to the floor. She reaches for the photograph, then freezes. Somewhere in the hallway, floorboards creak.

She opens the door. The hallway is empty, long and black. At the far end stands a closed door that has been locked since her mother died.

A brief flicker of lightning illuminates it.

Cut to black.

## SCENE 2 — MORNING DOES NOT EXPLAIN EVERYTHING

Pale morning light fills the kitchen. UNCLE EMEKA makes tea. Amara sits without touching her food.

**EMEKA**  
You were awake again.

**AMARA**  
Did you hear anything last night?

**EMEKA**  
Rain. The roof. This house complaining, as usual.

**AMARA**  
I heard Mama.

Emeka stops stirring. It lasts only a second, but Amara sees it.

**EMEKA**  
You were dreaming.

**AMARA**  
She said, “Don’t let him burn it.”

**EMEKA**  
Grief can borrow the voices we miss most.

**AMARA**  
Then why are you frightened?

He sets the spoon down too hard.

**EMEKA**  
Because you’re not sleeping. Because you’re searching for answers that won’t bring her back.

**AMARA**  
I’m not asking you to bring her back. I’m asking you what you did with her things.

Emeka looks toward the hallway.

**EMEKA**  
Some things are better left where they are.

He leaves. Amara watches the doorway long after he is gone.

## SCENE 3 — THE MESSAGE

At school, Amara sits beside NNEKA beneath a covered walkway. Rainwater drips from the roof.

**NNEKA**  
You look like you fought a ghost and lost.

**AMARA**  
I heard my mother.

Nneka’s smile fades.

**NNEKA**  
In a dream?

**AMARA**  
I was awake.

Amara’s phone vibrates. A message has arrived from a contact saved as MAMA. The number has been disconnected since the funeral.

The audio attachment is blank except for a tiny waveform. Amara presses play.

**MAMA IFEOMA (RECORDING)**  
My Amara. If you’re hearing this, I didn’t find the courage to tell you while I was alive. Go to the sewing room. Look beneath the blue cloth. And whatever your uncle says, listen to the whole recording.

The audio ends with a click.

Amara’s hands shake.

**NNEKA**  
That could be an old scheduled message.

**AMARA**  
She knew I would hear her last night.

**NNEKA**  
I don’t know what it is. But you shouldn’t go alone.

Amara looks up. Across the courtyard, Emeka stands beside the gate, watching her.

When she blinks, he is gone.

## SCENE 4 — THE LOCKED DOOR

That evening, Amara searches a drawer for the old key. She finds thread, buttons, a small brass thimble, and a photograph of her mother holding a blue cloth.

Beneath the sewing basket is a key wrapped in a strip of fabric.

Amara approaches the locked sewing-room door. She hesitates, then turns the key.

The room smells of cedar and dust. A sewing machine sits beneath a white sheet. Fabric hangs from the wall like still shadows. In the center of the room, a blue cloth covers a low wooden table.

Amara pulls it away. Beneath it is a tin box.

Inside: a cassette tape, a locket, and a folded note in her mother’s handwriting. The writing is too delicate to read in the dim light, so Amara takes it to the window.

The note says: **“If Emeka asks, you never found this.”**

A floorboard creaks behind her.

Emeka stands in the doorway.

**EMEKA**  
Put it back.

**AMARA**  
What is it?

**EMEKA**  
Something your mother wanted buried.

**AMARA**  
She told me to find it.

**EMEKA**  
Your mother was ill. She was frightened. She didn’t always know what she was saying.

**AMARA**  
You said she left nothing.

**EMEKA**  
I said put it back.

He steps into the room. Amara closes her fingers around the cassette and backs away.

**AMARA**  
What did you do?

Emeka’s face changes—not anger, but grief so old it has hardened.

**EMEKA**  
You have no idea what you’re asking.

**AMARA**  
Then tell me.

He cannot. He leaves the room, and the door remains open behind him.

## SCENE 5 — THE VOICE ON THE TAPE

Nneka helps Amara borrow a cassette player from an elderly neighbor. They sit on Amara’s bedroom floor with the door locked. The tape clicks, hisses, and begins.

**MAMA IFEOMA (RECORDING)**  
Amara, my love. I wanted to tell you the truth many times. Every time I tried, I saw how young you were, and I chose another day.

A pause. A breath.

**MAMA IFEOMA (RECORDING)**  
The night of the storm, I argued with your uncle. I had discovered that your father had left money for your future, money that was supposed to be kept for your education. It was gone. Emeka said he used it to save the family home. I believed him, until I found the bank papers.

Amara looks at Nneka.

**MAMA IFEOMA (RECORDING)**  
I was going to tell you. I was going to take you away. But before I could, there was an accident on the road. Emeka was driving. He says he tried to avoid a fallen tree. I never knew what happened in those last minutes. I only know he came home with my blood on his shirt and a story he has repeated ever since.

The tape crackles.

**MAMA IFEOMA (RECORDING)**  
Do not decide what happened just because you are angry. Find the letter in the garden. The truth is there. And if you ever hear my voice after this, remember: love is not the same as fear.

The tape ends.

Amara is crying silently.

**NNEKA**  
We need the letter.

**AMARA**  
I need to know whether he killed her.

**NNEKA**  
Then we find out. We don’t guess.

## SCENE 6 — THE GARDEN

Night. The garden glistens under a lantern. Amara and Nneka search near a stone bench. Amara remembers the old family photograph: her mother standing beside a young tree.

They find the tree, now grown thick. At its base, a flat stone sits slightly out of place. Beneath it is a metal envelope tin wrapped in plastic.

Inside is a letter, a bank statement, and a small memory card.

The letter is addressed to Amara.

**MAMA IFEOMA (V.O.)**  
If you are reading this, the truth has finally reached you. I am sorry that I left you with questions. I am sorry that the people who love you have made silence feel like safety.

Footsteps sound on the path. Emeka appears in the lantern light.

**EMEKA**  
Give it to me.

**AMARA**  
Did you take the money?

**EMEKA**  
I borrowed it.

**AMARA**  
You told everyone it was spent on the house.

**EMEKA**  
I was going to replace it.

**AMARA**  
And the accident?

Emeka looks toward the dark road beyond the garden.

**EMEKA**  
The rain was terrible. The road was flooded. Your mother and I were arguing. She reached for the steering wheel. The car slid. I tried to pull her free.

**AMARA**  
Why did you lie?

**EMEKA**  
Because I was ashamed. Because I thought if you knew how we were fighting, you would remember her as angry instead of loving.

**AMARA**  
You let me think I was the reason you never spoke about her.

**EMEKA**  
No. Never that.

**AMARA**  
You let me grieve a story you invented.

He lowers his head.

**EMEKA**  
I was a coward.

A wind moves through the leaves. The lantern flickers, though the air has gone still.

From somewhere behind Amara, her mother whispers.

**MAMA IFEOMA (V.O.)**  
My child.

Amara turns. No one is there.

## SCENE 7 — WHAT THE RECORDING CANNOT PROVE

Inside the house, Amara studies the memory card with Nneka. It contains a short video recorded by her mother weeks before the accident. The footage shows IFEOMA speaking directly to the camera, alive and tired.

**IFEOMA (VIDEO)**  
If you found this, then I failed to tell you myself. Emeka did take the money. But he did not take it for himself. He paid the hospital debt your father left behind and tried to hide it because he thought he could fix everything before anyone knew.

A pause. IFEOMA looks off-camera.

**IFEOMA (VIDEO)**  
That does not excuse the lie. It does not excuse the argument. But I don’t want you to build your life around hating him. I was angry that night. He was angry too. We were both tired. The storm made the road dangerous. I cannot tell you what happened in the seconds before the car left the road. I can only tell you that he tried to save me.

Amara stops the video. Her face is torn between relief and anger.

**NNEKA**  
So he didn’t murder her.

**AMARA**  
He still lied.

**NNEKA**  
Both can be true.

Amara looks at the locket. Inside is a tiny photograph of her as a baby, held by her mother.

**AMARA**  
I thought if I knew what happened, the pain would stop.

**NNEKA**  
Maybe knowing isn’t the same as healing.

## SCENE 8 — THE CONFESSION

Emeka sits at the kitchen table. Amara places the bank statement and letter in front of him.

**AMARA**  
You should have told me.

**EMEKA**  
Yes.

**AMARA**  
You should have let me be angry.

**EMEKA**  
Yes.

**AMARA**  
You made me afraid of the only memories I had left.

He cannot look at her.

**EMEKA**  
Every time I heard you laugh like her, I remembered that night. I thought if I kept the truth away from you, I could keep you away from the pain. I was wrong.

**AMARA**  
You weren’t protecting me. You were protecting yourself.

Emeka nods.

**EMEKA**  
I know.

Silence fills the room. For the first time, he does not try to fill it with an explanation.

**EMEKA**  
I can’t ask you to forgive me.

**AMARA**  
Good. Because I don’t know if I can.

She picks up the locket and leaves him at the table. He does not follow.

## SCENE 9 — GOODBYE, MAMA

Near dawn, Amara sits in the sewing room. The blue cloth is folded neatly beside her. The cassette player rests on the table.

She presses play on the final section of the tape, which she had not heard before.

**MAMA IFEOMA (RECORDING)**  
If I could leave you one thing, my love, it would be this: you do not owe the dead your sadness. You can miss me and still dance. You can remember me and still grow older. You can forgive someone one day, or never. Your life belongs to you.

Amara closes her eyes.

**MAMA IFEOMA (RECORDING)**  
And if you hear my voice in the rain, don’t be afraid. It may be memory. It may be love. It may be the house settling around you. You do not have to know which one to keep living.

The tape clicks off.

Amara presses the locket to her heart. She cries openly now, not trying to hide it.

**AMARA**  
I miss you.

The curtains stir. A soft warmth enters the room with the sunrise.

**MAMA IFEOMA (V.O.)**  
I know.

Amara opens her eyes. The room is empty. She smiles through tears.

## SCENE 10 — THE LAST WHISPER

Morning. Amara opens the windows. Birds call from the garden. Emeka stands in the doorway, carrying two cups of tea. He leaves one on the table without asking to be forgiven.

Amara looks at him. After a long moment, she takes the cup.

It is not forgiveness. It is a beginning.

Her phone lies face down on the table. The screen is dark. No one touches it.

A whisper drifts through the room, gentle and unmistakable.

**MAMA IFEOMA (V.O.)**  
Amara.

Amara turns toward the phone.

The screen lights up by itself. There is no message, no call, no notification—only the reflection of the open window.

A second whisper comes from behind her, not from the phone.

**MAMA IFEOMA (V.O.)**  
Look after him, too.

Amara turns. The doorway is empty.

She looks back at Emeka. He has heard nothing. He is staring into his tea, finally allowing himself to cry.

Amara takes her mother’s locket and steps into the sunlight.

Cut to black.

**END**

### End-credit card
*For everyone who has carried a question after losing someone they love.*

## Director’s note
Play the supernatural moments with restraint. The emotional mystery matters more than a visible ghost. Keep the mother’s voice warm and intimate, never monstrous. The final whisper should leave two interpretations open: a genuine presence, or grief giving Amara the courage to keep living.
